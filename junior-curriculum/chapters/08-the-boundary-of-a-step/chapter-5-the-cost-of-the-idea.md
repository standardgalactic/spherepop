# The Boundary of a Step

## Chapter 5: The Cost of the Idea

Ibo wanted to add every number from 1 to 100. He counted steps in two ways.

### Step 1: One number at a time

The slow way adds each number to a running total.

```
( 1 + 2 )  →  3
( 3 + 3 )  →  6
( 6 + 4 )  →  10
...
```

Each line is one pop of an addition, which is on the menu. How many are there? There are 100 numbers. The first line uses two of them, and every line after that adds one more. So there are **99 additions**.

### Step 2: The pairing way

In The Order of Discovery, Kofi paired the ends. The first and last make 101, and there are 50 such pairs.

```
( 1 + 100 )  →  101
( 50 × 101 )  →  5,050
```

That is **2 pops**: one addition and one multiplication, both on the menu.

99 steps against 2 steps. It looks like a huge difference.

### Step 3: But the idea was not free

Ibo then asked a fair question: *what did it cost to think of pairing?*

To write down those 2 pops, someone had to notice that the ends pair up, that every pair makes the same sum, and that there are exactly 50 pairs. None of that was counted as a step. It was an **idea**, and the idea took thinking.

So "2 steps" really means "2 steps on the menu, after one idea that is not on the menu." Even the recorded moves miss it. A redraw into pairs is one line in the trace, but the thinking that led to it is in neither count.

This is a lesson about counting. There are two honest ways to say how hard something is.

- **Steps on the menu**: how many pops once you know what to do.
- **The idea**: what you had to see before you could write the pops down.

For adding 1 to 100, the idea is valuable because it works for every list like it. Once Kofi has it, the next list costs two steps and no new idea.

### Step 4: The idea pays for itself

Try the same idea on 1 to 1,000.

- One at a time: **999 additions**.
- By pairs: ( 1 + 1,000 ) → 1,001, then ( 500 × 1,001 ) → 500,500. That is **2 pops**.

The slow way grew with the list. The pairing way did not. A good idea costs thought once and saves steps every time after.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What did both methods keep?** (The sum.)
- **What did the step count leave out?** (The thinking that came before the pops.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

A step count is trustworthy when it says what the menu is and says what ideas were used. "2 steps" alone is incomplete. "2 steps on this menu, after the idea of pairing" is honest. The sum itself is checked by the other method: 5,050 for 1 to 100 either way.

### Practice

1. How many additions does it take to add 1 to 10 one at a time? How many pops with pairing?

2. How many additions does it take to add 1 to 20 one at a time?

3. Use pairing to add 1 to 1,000. What is the sum?

4. Ibo says: "Pairing is 2 steps, so it is easy." What would you say to him?

5. Name another idea that cost thought once and saved steps later.

### Answers

1. One at a time: **9 additions**. With pairing: ( 1 + 10 ) → 11 and ( 5 × 11 ) → 55, which is **2 pops**.
2. There are 20 numbers, so **19 additions**.
3. 500 pairs of 1,001, so **500,500**.
4. Two steps once you know the idea. The idea itself is part of the cost, and it should be counted or named.
5. For example, the missing corner: seeing it once makes products like 48 × 52 easy afterward. Other answers work too.
