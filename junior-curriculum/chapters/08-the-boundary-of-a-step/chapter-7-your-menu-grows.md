# The Boundary of a Step

## Chapter 7: Your Menu Grows

Ibo, Sade and Tomas each did the sum 12 × 12 and wrote how many pops it took.

### Step 1: Three menus

**Tomas** knows his times tables up to 12 by heart. For him, 12 × 12 is on the menu.

```
( 12 × 12 )  →  144
```

**1 pop.**

**Sade** knows her tables up to 10. She needs to split.

```
( 12 × 12 )
redraw  →  ( ( 12 × 10 ) + ( 12 × 2 ) )
pop  →  ( 120 + 24 )
pop  →  144
```

**3 pops** (and one redraw).

**Ibo** is newer to multiplying. He only trusts adding for now, so he adds twelve twelves.

```
12 + 12 → 24 → 36 → 48 → 60 → 72 → 84 → 96 → 108 → 120 → 132 → 144
```

**11 pops.**

Three people, three counts: 1, 3 and 11. The sum is the same. The menus are different.

### Step 2: A menu is what you can do in one step

In The Boundary of a Step, we said a step is one pop of something on the menu. Now we see that the menu belongs to a person. It holds whatever that person can do in one go.

- For a beginner, the menu has addition.
- Later it adds the times tables.
- Later it adds tricks, such as the missing corner.

Each time something new joins the menu, the same problem takes fewer steps.

### Step 3: How a menu grows

Sade learned a new fact: 12 × 12 = 144. She did it by practice, over a few weeks. Now it is on her menu.

Did the calculation get simpler? Not for the problem. 12 × 12 is still the same product. What changed is Sade. What used to take three pops now takes one.

This is an important thing to say honestly. When someone says "that was easy," it may mean the problem is easy, or it may mean they have done it many times. A beginner and an expert can both be right about their own step counts.

### Step 4: Saying whose menu

So when you count steps, say whose menu you are using.

- "11 steps, for someone who only adds."
- "3 steps, with the times tables to 10."
- "1 step, if 12 × 12 is known."

This is the idea from chapter 5 again: a count with no menu named is incomplete.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What stayed the same for all three?** (The product, 144.)
- **What was different?** (How many steps, because the menus were different.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

A step count is trustworthy when it says what the menu is. Without that, "it took 3 steps" does not say whether the problem is easy or the person is experienced.

The product is checked the same way for everyone: 12 × 12 is 144 because 144 ÷ 12 = 12.

### Practice

1. Count the pops to find 7 × 8 for a person who only adds. For a person who knows the times tables.

2. Sade knows tables to 10. How many pops does she need for 13 × 6? (Split 13.)

3. Tomas knows tables to 12. How many pops does 12 × 11 take for him?

4. A beginner says "13 × 6 is hard" and an expert says it is easy. Can both be right?

5. Name something that was once hard for you and is now one step.

### Answers

1. For adding only: seven eights take **6 additions** (or eight sevens, 7 additions). With the tables: **1 pop**.
2. ( ( 10 × 6 ) + ( 3 × 6 ) ) → ( 60 + 18 ) → 78. That is **3 pops** (and one redraw).
3. He knows it: ( 12 × 11 ) → 132, **1 pop**.
4. Yes. The problem is the same, but the menus differ. "Hard" depends on whose menu.
5. Many answers work, such as a times table fact, a spelling, or tying shoes.
