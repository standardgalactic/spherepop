# The Boundary of a Step

## Chapter 3: Three Diaries of the Same Calculation

Sade, Ibo and Tomas each calculated 12 × 13. Each kept a diary. They all got the right answer, but their diaries looked very different.

### Step 1: Three diaries

**Ibo's diary** had one line:

```
( 12 × 13 )  →  156
```

**Sade's diary** had three lines:

```
( 12 × 13 )
redraw  →  ( ( 12 × 10 ) + ( 12 × 3 ) )
pop both  →  ( 120 + 36 )
pop  →  156
```

**Tomas's diary** wrote every single pop on its own line:

```
( 12 × 13 )
redraw  →  ( ( 12 × 10 ) + ( 12 × 3 ) )
pop ( 12 × 10 )  →  120
( 120 + ( 12 × 3 ) )
pop ( 12 × 3 )  →  36
( 120 + 36 )
pop  →  156
```

These three diaries describe the same calculation. They just zoom in to different levels. Ibo's zooms out so far that the whole calculation is one step. Tomas's zooms in so far that every pop is a step.

### Step 2: What does each diary let you see?

On Tuesday, a new student, Pip, calculated 12 × 13 and got 146. Pip's answer was wrong. The teacher wanted to find the mistake.

**If Pip had kept a diary like Ibo's:**

```
( 12 × 13 )  →  146
```

The mistake is somewhere, but the diary doesn't say where. All the teacher can do is repeat the whole calculation.

**If Pip had kept a diary like Sade's:**

```
( 12 × 13 )
redraw  →  ( ( 12 × 10 ) + ( 12 × 3 ) )
pop both  →  ( 120 + 26 )
pop  →  146
```

Now the teacher can see it. 120 is right, but ( 12 × 3 ) is 36, not 26. The mistake is on the third line, in the "pop both".

**If Pip had kept a diary like Tomas's**, the teacher would see the exact line: *pop ( 12 × 3 ) → 26*.

A finer diary pins down a mistake more precisely.

### Step 3: But finer is not always better

Tomas's diary is the longest. For a hard calculation with hundreds of steps, a diary that shows every pop would fill a whole notebook, and nobody would read it. A reader could easily lose the main idea in the details.

So there is a trade. A finer diary tells you more about *where*, and costs more time to write and read. A coarser diary is quick and clear about the big idea, and hides the details.

The right level depends on the job.

- A shopper adding up a bill needs only Ibo's level.
- A teacher looking for a mistake wants Sade's or Tomas's.
- A person teaching the trick, to show why 12 × 13 splits into 12 × 10 and 12 × 3, wants Sade's.

### Step 4: A step is a choice of zoom

In the last two chapters, we said a step is one pop. That is Tomas's level. But the word "step" depends on the diary.

- At Ibo's level, 12 × 13 is **1 step**.
- At Sade's level, it is **3 steps**.
- At Tomas's level, it is **4 steps**: one redraw and three pops.

For this reason, whenever someone says "that took 3 steps," a good question is **"3 steps at what level?"**. The answer is only meaningful when you know the zoom.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What do all three diaries keep?** (The question and the answer, 156.)
- **What does a finer diary keep that a coarser one drops?** (The place where a mistake could hide.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

A diary is trustworthy if each line can be checked by itself: the circles on the left and on the right have the same value. The coarse diary is no less trustworthy in what it says, but it gives you less to check.

When you have two diaries of the same level that disagree, there is a mistake in at least one. You can compare line by line to find where they first differ.

### Practice

1. Write Sade's level of diary for 15 × 12, splitting it as ( 15 × 10 ) + ( 15 × 2 ).

2. Pip wrote this diary: ( ( 15 × 10 ) + ( 15 × 2 ) ) → ( 150 + 20 ) → 170. Which line has the mistake? What should it be?

3. How many steps is 15 × 12 at Ibo's level? At Sade's level (your diary from problem 1)?

4. Name one job where a coarse diary is enough, and one where you would want a fine diary.

5. Two friends both say "it took 5 steps." One used a diary that writes every pop, and the other used one that groups pops. Could they still have done the same calculation? Explain.

### Answers

1. ( 15 × 12 ), then redraw to ( ( 15 × 10 ) + ( 15 × 2 ) ), then pop both to ( 150 + 30 ), then pop to **180**.
2. The mistake is in "( 15 × 2 )", which is 30, not 20. The line should be ( 150 + 30 ), giving 180.
3. At Ibo's level, **1 step**. At Sade's level, **3 lines** after the question (redraw, pop both, pop).
4. Many answers work. A coarse diary is enough for a quick shopping bill. A fine diary is better for finding an error in a test.
5. Yes. "Steps" depends on how finely you write the diary, so the same calculation can be 5 steps at one level and 3 at another. Only the same level compares fairly.
