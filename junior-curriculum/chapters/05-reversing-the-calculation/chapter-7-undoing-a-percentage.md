# Reversing the Calculation

## Chapter 7: Undoing a Percentage

A coat is on sale for 20% off. The sale price is **48** dollars. What was the original price?

Lena's first idea was to add 20% back.

### Step 1: The tempting wrong answer

20% of 48 is 9.6. Add it back:

```
( 48 + 9.6 )  →  57.6
```

Lena checked it. She took 20% off 57.6 and got 57.6 − 11.52 = 46.08. That is not 48. So 57.6 is wrong.

Why? The 20% was taken off the **original** price, not off 48. Twenty percent of the original is a bigger number than twenty percent of the sale price. Adding the wrong 20% back gives the wrong answer.

### Step 2: Write the percent as a button

Taking 20% off leaves 80% of the price. 80% is four fifths. So the "20% off" button is:

```
( ( original × 4 ) ÷ 5 )  =  48
```

Now Lena undoes the buttons in the opposite order, as in chapter 2.

- Undo ÷5: 48 × 5 = 240.
- Undo ×4: 240 ÷ 4 = 60.

The original price was **60** dollars. Check: 60 × 4 = 240, 240 ÷ 5 = 48. ✓

### Step 3: A rise instead of a fall

A game costs more now because the price went up by 25%. The new price is 75 dollars. What was it before?

A rise of 25% leaves 125% of the old price, which is five quarters.

```
( ( original × 5 ) ÷ 4 )  =  75
```

- Undo ÷4: 75 × 4 = 300.
- Undo ×5: 300 ÷ 5 = 60.

The old price was **60** dollars. Check: 60 + 25% of 60 is 60 + 15 = 75. ✓

### Step 4: Why the wrong way is tempting

"20% off" and "20% on" look like opposites. They are not. Take 20% off 60 and you get 48. Add 20% to 48 and you get 57.6, not 60.

```
( 60 − 12 )  →  48
( 48 + 9.6 )  →  57.6
```

A percent is a share of whatever number it is applied to. It takes different amounts from different numbers. So the button for "20% off" cannot be undone by "20% on". The undo button is a different percent: to get from 48 back to 60, you add 25% of 48.

```
( 48 + 12 )  →  60       (12 is 25% of 48)
```

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What does the undo keep?** (The original price, found exactly.)
- **What did the tempting method get wrong?** (Which number the percent was taken of.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

The reason is that "20% off" is exactly "multiply by 4, divide by 5", and every button of that kind can be undone. The check is to apply the original percent to the answer and see if you land on the sale price.

### Practice

1. After 10% off, a shirt costs 45 dollars. What was the original? (90% is nine tenths.)

2. After a rise of 50%, a ticket costs 90 dollars. What was the old price? (150% is three halves.)

3. After 25% off, a hat costs 30 dollars. What was the original?

4. After 20% off, a bag costs 36 dollars. What was the original?

5. Why is 30 + 25% of 30 not the answer to problem 3?

### Answers

1. ( ( original × 9 ) ÷ 10 ) = 45. Undo ÷10: 450. Undo ×9: 450 ÷ 9 = **50** dollars.
2. ( ( old × 3 ) ÷ 2 ) = 90. Undo ÷2: 180. Undo ×3: 180 ÷ 3 = **60** dollars.
3. 75% is three quarters. ( ( original × 3 ) ÷ 4 ) = 30. Undo ÷4: 120. Undo ×3: **40** dollars.
4. 80% is four fifths. Undo ÷5: 180. Undo ×4: 180 ÷ 4 = **45** dollars.
5. 30 + 25% of 30 = 37.50, but the 25% was taken off the original 40, not off 30. Twenty-five percent of 40 is 10, and 30 + 10 = 40.
