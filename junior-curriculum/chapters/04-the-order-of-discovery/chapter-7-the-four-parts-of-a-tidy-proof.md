# The Order of Discovery

## Chapter 7: The Four Parts of a Tidy Proof

By now Kofi has a messy notebook full of discoveries. When he wants to show a friend, he needs a tidy one. Kofi found that every good tidy proof has four parts.

Here is a claim he wants to share:

*Any three numbers in a row add up to three times the middle one.*

### Part 1: The claim

Say exactly what you are going to show. Kofi writes it as a plain sentence.

*If you add three numbers in a row, the total is the middle number times 3.*

The claim must be clear enough to test. "Three numbers in a row" means numbers such as 4, 5, 6, or 10, 11, 12.

### Part 2: The plan

Say, in a sentence, how you will show it. Kofi's plan:

*Give the middle number a name, then write the other two in terms of it.*

The plan is not the proof. It tells the reader where to look.

### Part 3: The steps

Now do it, one small step at a time. Kofi names the middle number m. The number before it is m − 1 and the number after it is m + 1.

The −1 and the +1 cancel, which is why naming the middle number was a good choice in The Choice Before the Calculation.

```
( ( m − 1 ) + m + ( m + 1 ) )
redraw  →  ( ( m + m + m ) + ( 1 − 1 ) )
pop ( 1 − 1 )  →  0
redraw  →  ( 3 × m )
```

Every step keeps the value, so the total equals 3 × m for any m.

### Part 4: The check

Finish by testing the claim on a case, so a reader sees it work. Kofi picks two.

```
4 + 5 + 6  =  15      and   3 × 5   =  15   ✓
10 + 11 + 12 = 33     and   3 × 11  =  33   ✓
```

A check does not prove the claim. The steps did that. The check protects against a slip in the steps, and it lets a reader see the claim with their own eyes.

### Step 5: What the tidy proof leaves out

The tidy proof left out the messy notebook: the first guesses, the dead ends, and the moment when Kofi noticed 5 was always in the middle of 4 and 6. As in chapter 3, the tidy notebook is for telling, and the messy notebook is for finding.

But a good tidy proof leaves a few things in:

- the **name** and what it stands for (m is the middle number),
- the **reason** for each step (every redraw keeps the value),
- the **check** at the end.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What did the four parts keep?** (The claim, the reason it is true, and a test of it.)
- **What did we drop?** (The order in which Kofi discovered it.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

A reader can follow each step on their own, so they need not take your word. The plan tells them where you are going, and the check shows them it works.

### Practice

1. Use the four parts to show that the sum of two numbers in a row is odd. (Name the first number n.)

2. Check your claim in problem 1 with 7 + 8 and 20 + 21.

3. Write the claim and plan only for: "Any four numbers in a row add to an even number." Do not do the steps.

4. Which part of a proof says what you are going to show?

5. Why is the check at the end not a proof?

### Answers

1. The numbers are n and n + 1. Their sum is ( n + n + 1 ), which is ( ( 2 × n ) + 1 ). Two lots of n is even, and adding 1 makes it odd.
2. 7 + 8 = 15, which is odd. ✓ 20 + 21 = 41, which is odd. ✓
3. Claim: "The sum of any four numbers in a row is even." Plan: "Name the first number n, write the four numbers in terms of n, and show the total is 2 times something."
4. The **claim**.
5. A check tests particular cases. The claim covers every case, and only the steps cover those.
