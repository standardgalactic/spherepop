# Solving by Changing the Question

## Chapter 8: From 48 × 52 to 50 × 50

Pia was given the case file card **48 × 52**. She did not answer it. She answered a *different* question, **50 × 50**, and then paid for the change.

This chapter is about what a change of question costs.

### Step 1: The slide and its price

Pia slid 48 up by 2 and 52 down by 2. The two numbers still add to 100.

```
( 48 × 52 )   →   ( 50 × 50 )
```

The new question has a different answer. 50 × 50 = 2,500, and 48 × 52 = 2,496. The change **added 4** to the answer. That is its price.

Pia can name the price before she finishes, because she knows how the numbers moved: each is 2 from the middle, so the price is 2 × 2.

```
( 48 × 52 )  =  ( ( 50 × 50 ) − ( 2 × 2 ) )
```

Change the question, solve the easier one, then take back the price. That is the whole trick.

### Step 2: A change whose price is hard

Pia tried a different slide: move *both* numbers up by 2.

```
( 48 × 52 )   →   ( 50 × 54 )
```

This is not any easier. 50 × 54 = 2,700, and the price is 2,700 − 2,496 = **204**. She can name it as well: 2 × 52 + 48 × 2 + 4. But the price is not a tidy square, so paying it is nearly as much work as the original.

| Change | New question | Price | Easy to pay? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slide opposite by 2 | 50 × 50 | 4 | Yes, 2 × 2 |
| Slide both up by 2 | 50 × 54 | 204 | No |

A good change has a price that is **small** and **easy to name**.

### Step 3: Every change has a price

Pia made a rule for her notebook: *before you change a question, ask what the change costs, and write it down.*

```
change   ( 48 × 52 )  →  ( 50 × 50 )
price    4  (the answer to the new question is 4 too high)
```

This is the same as an estimate and its debt in A Useful Wrong Answer. The only difference is that here Pia pays the debt at once, so the answer to the original question is exact.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What did the change keep?** (The sum of the two numbers, 100, and the shape of the problem.)
- **What did it lose?** (The exact answer to the original question, until the price was paid.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

Pia can say how much the answer changed because she knows how the numbers moved. The price is a calculation, not a hope. After she pays it, she checks the result by another route, such as 48 × 50 + 48 × 2 = 2,400 + 96 = 2,496.

### Practice

1. Change **49 × 51** to 50 × 50. What is the price? What is the answer to 49 × 51?

2. Change **38 × 42** to 40 × 40. What is the price? Find 38 × 42.

3. Change **23 × 27** to 25 × 25. Find 23 × 27.

4. Change **48 × 52** to 48 × 50 (only the second number moves). What is the price? Use it to find 48 × 52.

5. Pia says, "I changed the question, so my answer is wrong." Is she right?

### Answers

1. Each is 1 from the middle, so the price is 1 × 1 = **1**. 2,500 − 1 = **2,499**.
2. Each is 2 from the middle, so the price is **4**. 1,600 − 4 = **1,596**.
3. Each is 2 from the middle, so the price is 4. 625 − 4 = **621**.
4. 48 × 50 = 2,400 is too low by 48 × 2 = **96**. 2,400 + 96 = **2,496**.
5. Only if she forgets to pay the price. A changed question gives a different answer, but a named price turns it back into the answer to the original question.
