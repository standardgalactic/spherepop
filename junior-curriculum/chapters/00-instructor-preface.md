# Preface for Instructors

The circle-and-pop notation in these books comes from SpherePOP, a calculus in which popping is a commitment that is recorded in a history. The books use only a specialized arithmetic form of it. Popping here means completing a local calculation inside a ready circle and recording the result.

Three moves are kept distinct in every trace, even when they give the same answer:

- a **pop** completes a calculation and records the result;
- a **redraw** rewrites the problem without changing its value;
- a **refusal** records a move that was possible but not allowed, and why.

Once defined in the introduction, these work as an independent teaching language, and the books can be read without any knowledge of where they came from. Nothing in the children's text depends on the calculus, and no later use of the notation is assumed.

Two notes for classroom use:

- The recurring case, 48 × 52, appears in every book from a different side. It is a convenient anchor for discussion.
- Chapters differ in difficulty. The material on observational equivalence, irreversible operations, general proof, and the difference between possible and permitted moves works best with an adult discussing the examples alongside the child.
