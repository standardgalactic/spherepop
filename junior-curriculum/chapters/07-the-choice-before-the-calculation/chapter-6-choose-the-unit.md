# The Choice Before the Calculation

## Chapter 6: Choose the Unit

Rina's trips to the pool take 45 minutes each. She makes 4 trips a week. How long does she spend altogether?

She could calculate in hours or in minutes. She stopped to choose.

### Step 1: Two units, two calculations

**In hours.** 45 minutes is 0.75 hours.

```
( 4 × 0.75 )  →  3
```

**In minutes.** Stay with 45.

```
( 4 × 45 )  →  180
```

Then 180 minutes is 3 hours. Both give the same answer, 3 hours. The minutes version used whole numbers all the way, which many people find easier. The hours version needed a decimal.

### Step 2: Money in cents

Three notebooks cost 1.99 dollars each.

**In dollars.** ( 3 × 1.99 ) has a decimal that is awkward.

**In cents.** 1.99 dollars is 199 cents.

```
( 3 × 199 )
redraw  →  ( ( 3 × 200 ) − ( 3 × 1 ) )
pop  →  ( 600 − 3 )
pop  →  597
```

597 cents is **5.97 dollars**.

Choosing cents turned a decimal problem into a whole-number problem, and then a friendly number (200) helped with the correction.

### Step 3: Mixed units

Rina walks for 2.5 hours and then rests for 90 minutes. How long is that in total?

The units do not match, so she picks one.

```
2.5 hours  =  150 minutes
( 150 + 90 )  →  240 minutes  =  4 hours
```

If she had added 2.5 and 90 directly, the answer would be meaningless, because hours and minutes are different sizes. First choose the unit, then calculate.

### Step 4: What makes a unit good?

Rina's rules:

- Pick the unit that makes **all** the numbers whole, if you can.
- Make sure **all** the quantities use the **same** unit before adding or subtracting them.
- Convert back at the end, so the answer is in a unit people expect.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What did changing the unit keep?** (The real amount of time or money.)
- **What changed?** (The numbers we wrote, and how easy the arithmetic was.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

The reason is that a unit is only a size of measure, and converting is exact: 45 minutes is 0.75 hours, and 199 cents is 1.99 dollars. The answer in either unit describes the same thing.

The check is to convert the answer back and compare: 5.97 dollars is 597 cents, and 3 hours is 180 minutes. ✓

### Practice

1. Six trips of 15 minutes each. How many minutes? How many hours?

2. Five notebooks at 2.98 dollars each. Work in cents and give the total in dollars.

3. Rina cycles 1.25 hours and then walks 45 minutes. What is the total in minutes? In hours?

4. How many hours are in 3 days and 12 hours?

5. Why is it wrong to add 2.5 hours and 90 minutes as 2.5 + 90?

### Answers

1. ( 6 × 15 ) → **90 minutes**, which is **1.5 hours**.
2. ( 5 × 298 ) = ( ( 5 × 300 ) − ( 5 × 2 ) ) = 1,500 − 10 = 1,490 cents, which is **14.90 dollars**.
3. 1.25 hours is 75 minutes. ( 75 + 45 ) → **120 minutes**, which is **2 hours**.
4. 3 days is 72 hours. 72 + 12 = **84 hours**.
5. Hours and minutes are different sizes, so 2.5 + 90 adds unlike things and gives nonsense.
