# What Must Remain Unresolved

## Chapter 4: Saying What You Do Know

Tavi still had not opened the jar. But her sister found a note on the lid: *between 5 and 9 marbles.*

Now Tavi knows something, but not everything. She wanted to say what she could without pretending.

### Step 1: A range for the jar

The jar x holds at least 5 and at most 9 marbles. The bags still hold 3 × 4 = 12.

```
( x + ( 3 × 4 ) )
pop ( 3 × 4 )  →  12
( x + 12 )
```

Tavi is stuck on x, but she can still say something true about the total. Try the smallest and largest jar.

```
smallest jar:  ( 5 + 12 )  →  17
largest jar:   ( 9 + 12 )  →  21
```

The total is **between 17 and 21**.

That is a good note. It does not claim to know the jar, and it does not throw away the clue. It is faithful, as in chapter 3, and more useful than "I don't know."

### Step 2: Different circles, different ranges

Tavi tried other circles with the same jar.

**( x × 4 ):** four lots of the jar. Smallest 5 × 4 = 20, largest 9 × 4 = 36. So the answer is between **20 and 36**.

**( x + y ):** two jars, each between 5 and 9. Smallest 5 + 5 = 10, largest 9 + 9 = 18. So between **10 and 18**.

### Step 3: Watch out for a flip

Then Tavi tried a circle where the jar is subtracted:

```
( 30 − x )
```

The smallest jar gives the **largest** answer.

```
smallest jar (5):  ( 30 − 5 )  →  25
largest jar (9):   ( 30 − 9 )  →  21
```

So ( 30 − x ) is between **21 and 25**. If Tavi had just used the old order, 5 for the small end and 9 for the big end, she would have written "between 25 and 21", which makes no sense.

The rule: **try both ends of the jar, and let the answers tell you which is small and which is large.**

### Step 4: A range is a kind of residual

In chapter 1, the note to self was ( x + 12 ). Here the note has two parts: the expression ( x + 12 ), and the clue about x. Together they say: "17 to 21 marbles, and I'll know more when the jar opens."

When the jar is opened and x turns out to be 7, Tavi pops ( 7 + 12 ) to 19. That is inside the range 17 to 21, so the range was honest.

### What did we keep?

Write the answers in your notebook.

- **What did the range keep?** (A true statement about every jar that fits the clue.)
- **What did it leave unknown?** (Exactly which jar it is.)

### What makes this trustworthy?

A range is trustworthy when it is built from the clue and both ends: the smallest and largest jar. It is tested when the jar opens. A value outside the range means a mistake in the clue or the arithmetic.

### Practice

1. The jar x holds between 3 and 8. What is the range of ( x + 12 )?

2. The jar x holds between 5 and 9. What is the range of ( x × 3 )?

3. The jar x holds between 5 and 9. What is the range of ( 50 − x )?

4. The jar x holds between 5 and 9, and the jar y holds between 2 and 4. What is the range of ( x + y )?

5. The jar opens and x is 12. The note said "between 5 and 9". What does that tell you?

### Answers

1. 3 + 12 = 15 and 8 + 12 = 20, so **15 to 20**.
2. 5 × 3 = 15 and 9 × 3 = 27, so **15 to 27**.
3. 50 − 9 = 41 and 50 − 5 = 45, so **41 to 45**.
4. 5 + 2 = 7 and 9 + 4 = 13, so **7 to 13**.
5. The note was wrong. Someone made a mistake about the clue, and it is time to check where.
