\chapterhead{2}{Features That Matter}{Picking a feature that really separates things}\label{ch2}

Some features split a pile in a useful way and some do not. Here is the tray of eight made-up bugs again.

\bugtray

Look at the bugs. A feature is a \textbf{good splitter} when some bugs have it and some do not, and when everyone can tell which is which.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.2cm}p{3.6cm}p{3.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{How many bugs have it} & \textbf{Good splitter?}\\
\midrule
Has wings & 4 of 8 & yes, a clear 4 and 4\\
Has spots & 4 of 8 & yes\\
Has long antennae & 4 of 8 & yes\\
Has six legs & 8 of 8 & no, it splits nothing\\
Is ``nice looking'' & depends on who looks & no, people disagree\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{tryit}{Rate the features}
For each feature, count how many of the eight bugs have it. Then say whether it is a good splitter.\par\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Feature} & \textbf{Bugs that have it} & \textbf{Good splitter? (yes/no)}\\ \hline
Wings & & \\ \hline
Spots & & \\ \hline
Long antennae & & \\ \hline
Six legs & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{tryit}

\begin{worked}{Check with real counts}
Wings: A, B, E, F have wings, so 4 of 8. Spots: A, C, E, G, so 4 of 8. Long antennae: A, B, G, H, so 4 of 8. Six legs: all 8.\\
A feature that every bug has, or no bug has, cannot split the pile. It keeps nothing about the difference between bugs.
\end{worked}

\begin{multix}[hmm]\multixbot[hmm]\tcblower
The best feature is the one most bugs have, because then I can sort most of them at once. Six legs has all eight bugs, so it is the best of all. Is it?
\end{multix}

\trust{We counted each feature on every bug and checked that people could tell who has it.}
{Wings, spots and long antennae each gave a clear split of 4 and 4.}
{That these are the \emph{best} features for every purpose. A feature matters only for a particular question.}

\begin{keepbox}
We chose wings to sort the bugs. \textbf{We kept} \wline[3.4cm] \textbf{and we set aside} \wline[3.4cm].
\end{keepbox}

\subhead{A clear feature, or a fuzzy one}
``Is it big?'' is fuzzy, because people will disagree about a middle-sized bug. ``Is it longer than 2~cm?'' is clear, because anyone with a ruler gets the same answer. Book~1 showed how a ruler and a rule turn a vague idea into a clear one.

\begin{tryit}{Make a fuzzy question clear}
Write each fuzzy question as a clear one you could check with a ruler or by counting.\par\medskip
``Is it big?'' \quad $\to$ \wline[6cm]\\[10pt]
``Is it long?'' \quad $\to$ \wline[6cm]\\[10pt]
``Does it have lots of spots?'' \quad $\to$ \wline[5cm]
\end{tryit}

\begin{worked}{Sample rewrites}
``Is it big?'' becomes ``Is it wider than 2~cm?''\\
``Does it have lots of spots?'' becomes ``Does it have more than 3 spots?''
\end{worked}
