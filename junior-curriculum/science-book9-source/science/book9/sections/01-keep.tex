\chapterhead{1}{What a Model Keeps}{A small stand-in that shrinks every length by the same amount}\label{ch1}

A toy car, a cardboard house, a street map and a drawing of the heart are all \textbf{models}. A model is a small stand-in for something big, hard to reach or hard to see. It keeps some things about the real thing and drops the rest. That is why it is useful, and also why it can mislead.

A \textbf{scale model} keeps one thing carefully: every length is shrunk by the \emph{same} number. If $1$~cm on the model stands for $50$~cm in the room, a $400$~cm wall becomes $400\div50=8$~cm. We write the scale as $1:50$.

\begin{center}
\sffamily\small\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.5}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.4cm}p{3.7cm}p{3.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Model} & \textbf{What it keeps} & \textbf{What it drops}\\
\midrule
A cardboard room & sizes and places & colour, texture, weight\\
A scale & the same shrink for every length & very small details\\
A measurement & a number to the nearest cm & the last few mm\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{tryit}{Shrink a room}
\textbf{You need:} a tape measure, a helper, a pencil and plain paper.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Pick a room. Measure its length and width, the width of a door and the length of the bed or the sofa.
\item Divide each real length by 50 to get the length on a $1:50$ model.
\item Draw the room on paper using the model lengths.
\end{enumerate}
\par\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Thing} & \textbf{Real length (cm)} & \textbf{Model length (cm)}\\ \hline
Room length & & \\ \hline
Room width & & \\ \hline
Door width & & \\ \hline
Bed or sofa length & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{tryit}
\adult{Stay clear of stairs and keep the tape low. Two people can hold the tape flat along the floor.}

\begin{worked}{Practice numbers, not from a real room}
Room length 400~cm, so the model is $400\div50=8$~cm. Room width 300~cm, so $6$~cm. Door 80~cm, so $1.6$~cm. Bed 200~cm, so $4$~cm.\\
Check one backwards: $4\times50=200$.
\end{worked}

\begin{multix}[hmm]\multixbot[hmm]\tcblower
The model room is 8~cm wide, so the real room is 8~cm wide. A model is the room, only smaller. I have been very sure about this. Am I right?
\end{multix}

\trust{We measured each real length twice, divided each by the same number and checked one of them backwards.}
{Every length was shrunk by the same amount, so the shapes and the proportions of the room are kept.}
{Anything we left out, such as the colour of the walls or how the bed feels, and any question the model was not built to answer.}

\begin{keepbox}
We turned a room into a drawing. \textbf{We kept} \wline[3.4cm] \textbf{and we set aside} \wline[3.4cm].
\end{keepbox}

\begin{tryit}{Two scales}
Shrink the same three things at two scales: $1:50$ and $1:20$. A smaller number after the colon means a bigger model.\par\medskip
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.6}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Thing} & \textbf{Real (cm)} & \textbf{At 1:50} & \textbf{At 1:20}\\ \hline
Bed & & & \\ \hline
Desk & & & \\ \hline
Door & & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{tryit}

\begin{worked}{Practice numbers}
Bed 200~cm: 4~cm at $1:50$ and 10~cm at $1:20$. Desk 120~cm: 2.4~cm and 6~cm. Door 80~cm: 1.6~cm and 4~cm.\\
The $1:20$ model shows more detail but needs more paper. The 400~cm room would be 20~cm long at $1:20$.
\end{worked}

\begin{puzzle}{Shrink it}
A sofa is 200~cm long. At $1:50$ it is \wline[1.6cm]~cm long.
\end{puzzle}

\begin{puzzle}{Grow it}
A model rug is 3~cm by 2~cm at $1:50$. The real rug is \wline[1.6cm]~cm by \wline[1.6cm]~cm.
\end{puzzle}

\begin{puzzle}{Which is bigger?}
Which model of the same bed is bigger, $1:20$ or $1:50$? \wline[2cm] Why? \wline[4cm]
\end{puzzle}

\begin{puzzle}{Kept or dropped?}
Write K for kept or D for dropped in a $1:50$ cardboard room.\\
(a) the length of the bed \wline[1cm] \quad (b) the colour of the bed \wline[1cm] \quad (c) where the door is \wline[1cm]\\
(d) how soft the bed is \wline[1cm] \quad (e) where the desk stands \wline[1cm]
\end{puzzle}

\begin{puzzle}{Unfair model}
Ana made the bed at $1:50$ and the desk at $1:20$. What goes wrong when she puts them in the same room? \wline[6cm]
\end{puzzle}

\begin{puzzle}{More shrinking}
